# Findings: what correlates with what, and does anything actually predict markets

Generated by `run_analysis.py` over the whole `data/` directory.
**133 series, 1,293 months, 1919-01 → 2026-09.** Every number below comes from
`summary.json` and the CSVs in `tables/`; every chart is in `charts/`.

Reproduce:

```bash
.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pip install -r findings/requirements-analysis.txt
.venv/Scripts/python.exe findings/run_analysis.py
```

Methods used: ADF + KPSS stationarity, Pearson and Spearman correlation with
Newey–West (HAC) standard errors, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across every test
family, hierarchical clustering, PCA, cross-correlation at lags 1–6, HAC-robust
Granger causality, Engle–Granger cointegration, rolling-window regime analysis,
event studies with a stationary block bootstrap, walk-forward out-of-sample
regression, and Ljung–Box tests on returns vs volatility.

---

## TL;DR

1. **Markets are strongly swayed — but only in the same month the news lands.**
   VIX, the dollar, financial stress and inflation breakevens each move with
   equities at |r| = 0.2–0.6. All of it is contemporaneous.
2. **Almost nothing predicts next month.** Of 276 lead/lag tests into equities,
   **exactly one survives FDR correction** (Chicago financial conditions,
   r = −0.19). Of 45 walk-forward out-of-sample models, **6 beat "assume the
   historical average", and the best one by 2.2% of variance.**
3. **Causality runs the other way.** Granger tests find equities predicting
   macro data (**7 significant targets**) more than macro data predicting
   equities (**2**). The stock market is a leading indicator, not a follower.
4. **World events barely register.** Geopolitical-risk spikes produce no
   statistically distinguishable equity move in the same month or any month
   after. Financial-stress spikes produce a −5.2% same-month hit and nothing
   afterwards.
5. **Direction is unpredictable; size is extremely predictable.** Return
   autocorrelation dies after lag 1; |return| autocorrelation is +0.16 even at
   lag 12 with Ljung–Box p ≈ 0. This is the sharpest split in the dataset.
6. **The sign of a correlation is not a constant.** Equities vs 10y yields ran
   −0.25 in the 1970s, +0.44 in the 2010s, −0.34 in the 2020s.

So: not random, but not exploitable either. The information is in the price by
the time the month closes.

## 0. Two methodological traps that invalidate naive answers

### Levels are non-stationary, so correlating them is meaningless

ADF + KPSS on the 47 core series: **9 of 47 levels are stationary, 44 of 47
changes are.** Correlating levels therefore mostly measures "both went up."

| pair | r on levels | r on monthly changes | HAC t | HAC p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nasdaq vs M2 | **+0.93** | +0.05 | +0.90 | 0.37 |
| Nasdaq vs Case-Shiller house prices | **+0.91** | +0.02 | +0.32 | 0.75 |
| Nasdaq vs Gold | **+0.92** | −0.01 | −0.21 | 0.83 |
| Nasdaq vs US CPI | **+0.80** | −0.07 | −1.24 | 0.22 |
| Nasdaq vs nonfarm payrolls | **+0.71** | −0.06 | −0.91 | 0.36 |
| Bitcoin vs Fed balance sheet | **+0.62** | +0.06 | +0.98 | 0.33 |

Every headline of the form "money printing drives stocks, r = 0.9" is this
table's first column. Chart: `charts/01_levels_vs_changes.png`.

### Monthly-average price series manufacture predictability

`sp500_monthly.csv` (Shiller) is a monthly **average** of daily closes. Its own
AR(1) is **+0.27**; a true month-end index (Nasdaq) is **+0.10**. Running
lead/lag tests against the averaged series produced a clean-looking but entirely
fake result in an earlier pass ("Nasdaq predicts next-month S&P, r = +0.50").

Consequence: the repo has **no long month-end S&P series**. FRED's `SP500` and
`DJIA` mirrors are truncated to a rolling 10 years (121 months, from 2016-09).
**Nasdaq Composite (month-end, 1971→, 667 months) is the equity proxy used
throughout this report.** `panel.py` documents this.

## 1. Data-quality findings for the repo itself

### Redundant series: 133 collected, ~122 independent

17 pairs correlate at |r| ≥ 0.95 *on monthly changes*, forming 5 groups
(`tables/16_redundant_groups.csv`):

| group | series |
|---|---|
| WTI, twice | `WTI crude spot` (market_drivers/FRED) = `WTI (long)` (oil_wti_daily) — **r = 1.000** |
| Brent, twice | `Brent crude spot` = `Brent (long)` — **r = 1.000** |
| TPU family | `TPU`, `TPU_RAW`, `TPU_SHARE`, `TPUD_index`, `TPUD_ARTICLES`, `TPUD_index_MA7` — six views of one index, r = 0.93–1.00 |
| EPU | `EPU global` ≈ `EPU Singapore`, r = 0.967 (the global aggregate appears to be mis-scoped) |
| long end | `US 10-year yield` ≈ `US 30-year yield`, r = 0.955 (genuine, not a bug) |

The oil duplication is worth fixing or documenting — it silently double-weights
oil in any correlation matrix built from the repo. The `EPU global` /
`EPU Singapore` near-identity looks like a scope-labelling bug in the EPU
collector and should be checked.

### Truncated series

| series | months | starts |
|---|---|---|
| `S&P 500 index` | 121 | 2016-09 |
| `Dow Jones Industrial` | 121 | 2016-09 |
| `Overnight reverse repo` | 182 | 2003-02 |
| TPU daily family | 141 | 2015-01 |

`US high-yield OAS` and `US investment-grade OAS` have **37 monthly
observations from 2023-09** — FRED publishes these from 1996. These are the two
best risk-appetite series available for free and they are currently unusable.
This is the highest-value collector fix in the repo.

Suggested addition to `build.py --check`: fail if a series' start date moves
forward between builds.

## 2. The correlation structure (same month)

**8,367 pairs tested; 1,184 significant after FDR** (≈418 would be expected by
chance at an uncorrected p < 0.05 — which is exactly why the correction matters).
Full table: `tables/04_contemporaneous_all_pairs.csv`. Near-duplicates excluded
from what follows.

### Big (|r| > 0.7) — structural, not informative

```
+0.95  US 10y  <-> US 5y yield          -0.88  payrolls <-> unemployment
+0.95  US 2y   <-> US 5y yield          -0.86  USD broad <-> USD/EUR
+0.91  Brent   <-> WTI                  -0.85  USD broad <-> AUD
+0.91  GPR     <-> GPR threats          +0.80  CAD/USD  <-> USD broad
+0.87  US 10y  <-> US 2y yield          +0.75  Bitcoin  <-> Ethereum
```

These are definitional: the yield curve is one factor, the dollar is one factor,
crude is one commodity, crypto is one asset.

### Medium (0.3–0.7) — the actual market mechanism

| r | pair | n |
|---|---|---|
| **−0.58** | VIX ↔ equities | 440 |
| **−0.53** | USD broad index ↔ equities | 248 |
| **−0.46** | St. Louis financial stress ↔ equities | 393 |
| **+0.44** | 10y inflation breakeven ↔ equities | 284 |
| **+0.40** | 5y breakeven ↔ equities | 284 |
| −0.37 | CAD per USD ↔ equities | 667 |
| **−0.29** | 10y **real** yield ↔ Gold | 283 |
| **+0.34** | CFTC gold positioning ↔ gold price | 487 |
| −0.28 | USD broad ↔ Gold | 247 |
| −0.39 | USD broad ↔ WTI | 248 |

Two clean directional stories:

- **Oil → CPI.** +0.29 same month, and **+0.55 with oil leading by one month**
  (n = 485). The strongest genuine lead/lag relationship in the entire panel.
- **Real yields → gold, not nominal yields.** Real 10y (TIPS) vs gold = −0.29;
  nominal 10y vs gold = **+0.00**. If you only look at nominal rates you conclude
  gold ignores rates. It doesn't — it ignores inflation expectations.

### Small but real (0.1–0.3)

EPU ↔ VIX +0.21 · consumer sentiment ↔ equities +0.15 · CFTC S&P positioning ↔
equities +0.14 · jobless claims ↔ equities −0.12 · Bitcoin ↔ equities +0.28.

### Essentially zero

Geopolitical risk ↔ oil **+0.03** · GPR ↔ gold **+0.08** · GPR ↔ VIX **+0.07** ·
gold ↔ equities **−0.01** · M2 ↔ CPI **−0.08** · supply-chain pressure ↔ CPI
**+0.05**.

The geopolitical result is the surprising one and it is robust across all three
GPR variants (risk, threats, acts) and 500 months. **Geopolitical events do not
move markets in any detectable average sense.** They move them when — and only
when — they route through financial conditions or oil supply, which the index
does not distinguish.

### Cluster structure

Six clusters (`tables/05_clusters.csv`, `charts/02_correlation_heatmap.png`,
`charts/03_dendrogram.png`):

1. **Risk-off**: VIX, MXN, CAD, USD broad, jobless claims, EPU, financial stress, recession probability, Fed assets, M2, supply chain, S&P positioning
2. **Growth/rates**: yields, curve spreads, breakevens, CPI, payrolls, IP, housing, sentiment, crude, PE10, equities
3. **Gold complex**: gold, gold positioning, EUR
4. **Geopolitics**: GPR risk / threats / acts — *its own cluster, linked to nothing else*
5. **Crypto**: Bitcoin + dollar positioning
6. 10y-note positioning, alone

That geopolitics forms an isolated cluster is the single most telling picture in
the analysis.

### PCA: no single "market factor"

46 series, 180 complete months. **PC1 = 18.7%, PC2 = 10.4%, PC3 = 8.6%; top 3 =
37.7%.** You need **16 of 46 components to reach 80%**. PC1 loads on oil, valuation and
financial-conditions indices (a risk-appetite factor); PC2 on labour and
activity; PC3 on the yield curve; PC4 on the dollar. Chart: `charts/04_pca.png`.

Interpretation: the world does *not* reduce to one driver. Risk appetite, the
real economy, the curve and the dollar are four separate things.

### Network

32 core series, 66 links at |r| ≥ 0.35 (`charts/10_network.png`). Most-connected
nodes: **St. Louis financial stress (12 links)**, 10y breakeven (11), USD broad
index (10). Financial stress is the hub — it is the channel through which
everything else touches everything else.

## 3. Does anything push anything else? (lead/lag + Granger)

**12,960 lead/lag tests, 502 significant after FDR** (`tables/07_leadlag_all.csv`).
Nearly all of the strong ones are *within* macro, and are mechanical rather than
causal — a weekly series predicting the monthly series built from it:

```
-0.865  jobless claims (t)  ->  nonfarm payrolls (t+1)
+0.841  jobless claims (t)  ->  unemployment rate (t+1)
+0.586  CPI (t)             ->  core CPI (t+1)
+0.549  WTI (t)             ->  CPI (t+1)          <- this one is real economics
-0.522  financial stress (t)->  payrolls (t+1)
```

### Into equities: 276 tests, **1 survivor**

| r | driver | lag | n | OLS t | HAC t | survives FDR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **−0.192** | Chicago financial conditions | 1m | 667 | −5.03 | **−5.23** | **yes** |
| −0.182 | 10y real yield | 3m | 281 | −3.09 | −3.50 | no |
| +0.144 | Bitcoin | 1m | 175 | +1.91 | +2.33 | no |
| +0.110 | VIX | 6m | 434 | +2.30 | +2.23 | no |
| −0.102 | Sticky CPI | 1m | 667 | −2.66 | −2.49 | no |
| +0.096 | Geopolitical acts | 1m | 499 | +2.16 | +1.98 | no |

And the one survivor is partly circular: the Chicago index is *built from* asset
prices, so "financial conditions predict equities" is partly "equities predict
equities."

Chart `charts/05_same_vs_next_month.png` puts the two side by side: every blue
bar (same month) reaches out past the noise band; almost every orange bar
(next month) sits inside it.

### Granger causality: the arrow points out of markets, not into them

2,162 HAC-robust tests, 220 significant after FDR (`tables/10_granger.csv`).

| direction | significant targets |
|---|---|
| macro → **equities** | **2** (Chicago FCI p_fdr = 0.000, Bitcoin p_fdr = 0.025) |
| **equities** → macro | **7** (PE10, consumer sentiment, jobless claims, industrial production, housing starts, equity-market uncertainty, …) |

Equities Granger-cause consumer sentiment (F = 54.7), jobless claims (F = 31.3),
industrial production (F = 22.6) and housing starts (F = 19.9). The market is
ahead of the data, not reacting to it. Anyone waiting for the macro print to
decide is reading the market's own past.

### Cointegration (long-run level relationships)

`tables/11_cointegration.csv`, Engle–Granger:

| pair | p | cointegrated |
|---|---|---|
| 30y mortgage rate ~ 10y yield (β = 1.00) | 0.003 | **yes** |
| 10y ~ 2y yield (β = 0.86) | 0.036 | **yes** |
| Nasdaq ~ CPI | 1.000 | no |
| Nasdaq ~ M2 | 0.974 | no |
| Gold ~ CPI | 0.965 | no |
| Gold ~ USD | 0.931 | no |
| S&P PE10 ~ 10y yield | 0.472 | no |

The yield curve and the mortgage spread are genuine long-run equilibria. **Gold
is not cointegrated with CPI over 955 months** — the "gold is an inflation hedge"
claim does not hold in level terms in this data. Neither stocks nor gold are
tied to the money supply in any equilibrium sense.

*Caveat:* Bitcoin ~ Nasdaq (p = 0.010) and Gold ~ Bitcoin (p = 0.015) also test
as cointegrated, but over 176–177 strongly trending months. Engle–Granger over a
short trending sample false-positives easily; treat both as unproven.

## 4. Event studies: what a shock actually does

Shock = level of the indicator more than 1.5 SD above its trailing 5-year mean.
Means with block-bootstrap 95% CIs, FDR-corrected across the family
(`tables/13_event_study.csv`, `charts/07_event_study.png`).

| shock | n | same month | next month | next 3 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **St. Louis financial stress** | 42 | **−5.19%** (p_fdr 0.000) | +0.19% | −0.67% |
| **US equity-market uncertainty** | 30 | **−3.75%** (p_fdr 0.044) | +1.23% | +5.11% |
| **VIX** | 47 | **−3.65%** (p_fdr 0.026) | +1.06% | +4.16% |
| **Chicago financial conditions** | 72 | **−2.27%** (p_fdr 0.044) | −1.09% | +0.26% |
| US economic policy uncertainty | 40 | −2.71% (p_fdr 0.095) | +0.41% | +1.08% |
| Geopolitical risk | 29 | −0.97% (ns) | +0.71% | +1.84% |
| Geopolitical threats | 39 | −0.80% (ns) | +0.89% | +2.43% |
| Geopolitical acts | 30 | −0.20% (ns) | +2.08% | +1.42% |
| Supply chain pressure | 47 | +0.75% (ns) | +0.84% | +0.63% |
| Unemployment rate | 112 | +0.62% (ns) | +0.34% | +1.10% |
| Initial jobless claims | 76 | −0.17% (ns) | −0.01% | +1.26% |

Read the columns, not the rows:

- **Same month: 4 of 11 shocks are significant, all financial, all negative.**
- **Next month: 0 of 11 significant. Next 3 months: 0 of 11.**
- **None of the three geopolitical measures is significant at any horizon.**

The apparent post-shock bounce (VIX +4.2% over the following quarter) has a CI
of [+0.25, +11.89] and does not survive correction. It is the well-known
"volatility spikes near local bottoms" pattern, too noisy to be a finding.

## 5. The predictability test that settles it

Walk-forward, expanding window, 180-month minimum training, one predictor at a
time, 45 predictors (`tables/14_out_of_sample.csv`,
`charts/08_out_of_sample_r2.png`). Benchmark = the historical mean return, which
is what "no skill" looks like.

```
predictors tested         45
beat the benchmark         6
median OOS R²         -0.51%
best OOS R²           +2.22%   Chicago financial conditions
worst OOS R²          -6.99%   Fed balance sheet
median sign accuracy   60.8%
base rate of up months 60.0%   <- the models are not calling direction,
                                  they are reproducing the drift
```

Median sign accuracy 60.8% against a 60.0% base rate of up months. **The models
are predicting "up" and collecting the equity risk premium.** That is not
forecasting.

Note the in-sample vs out-of-sample gap: predictors with in-sample |r| of 0.1–0.2
mostly deliver *negative* OOS R². Fitting a relationship and using it are
different activities.

## 6. What *is* predictable: volatility, not direction

Ljung–Box on the equity return series, 667 months
(`tables/15_return_vs_vol_predictability.csv`,
`charts/09_return_vs_volatility_autocorr.png`):

| series | lag 1 | lag 3 | lag 6 | lag 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **return** | ac +0.102, p = 0.008 | +0.010, p = 0.048 | +0.019, **p = 0.22** | −0.006, **p = 0.33** |
| **\|return\|** | +0.214, p ≈ 0 | +0.178, p ≈ 0 | +0.188, **p ≈ 0** | +0.162, **p ≈ 0** |
| squared return | +0.167, p ≈ 0 | +0.172, p ≈ 0 | +0.139, p ≈ 0 | +0.140, p ≈ 0 |

Direction is unforecastable past one month. **Size is forecastable a year out.**
Quiet markets stay quiet, violent markets stay violent. This is the one thing in
the dataset with real out-of-sample structure, and it is why the tradeable
industry built on this data is options and risk management, not stock picking.

Distribution of monthly equity returns: skew **−0.81**, excess kurtosis
**+2.71**, Jarque–Bera p = 1e-60. **8 months beyond 3 SD where a normal
distribution predicts 1.8.** Worst month −31.8%, best +19.9%. Downside tails are
fatter than upside.

## 7. Regimes: the correlations themselves move

`tables/12_regimes_by_decade.csv`, `charts/06_rolling_correlations.png`.

| pair | 1970s | 1980s | 1990s | 2000s | 2010s | 2020s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **equities vs 10y yield change** | −0.25 | −0.20 | −0.19 | **+0.28** | **+0.44** | **−0.34** |
| equities vs VIX | — | — | −0.47 | −0.64 | −0.71 | −0.64 |
| equities vs dollar | — | — | — | −0.59 | −0.55 | −0.50 |
| gold vs real yield | — | — | — | −0.29 | −0.33 | −0.28 |
| bitcoin vs equities | — | — | — | — | +0.13 | **+0.55** |
| gold vs equities | −0.10 | +0.15 | −0.07 | −0.03 | −0.08 | +0.10 |

The stock/bond relationship **changes sign twice** over the sample. In an
inflation regime, rising yields are bad for stocks; in a growth-scare regime,
they are good. A single full-sample number for this pair (≈ 0) is worse than
useless — it averages two opposite truths into a false one.

Bitcoin's transformation is the other headline: an uncorrelated curiosity at
+0.13 in the 2010s, a leveraged Nasdaq proxy at +0.55 in the 2020s. Whatever
diversification argument existed for it, this data no longer supports.

Stable: equities/VIX, equities/dollar, gold/real-yield. Those three are
mechanism, not fashion.

## 8. So: is it random?

No — and yes, in different senses.

**Not random:** market moves are highly structured *within the month they
happen*. Four financial indicators each explain 19–33% of monthly equity
variance (VIX 33%, dollar 28%, financial stress 21%, breakevens 19%). Risk appetite, the real economy, the curve and the dollar are four distinct
identifiable factors. Volatility is strongly autocorrelated a year out. Oil
pushes CPI up with a one-month lag. Real yields push gold down. None of that is
noise.

**Effectively random:** the *next* move. 276 lead/lag tests into equities yield
one survivor and it is partly circular. 45 out-of-sample models yield a median
R² of −0.51%. 11 shock types yield zero significant follow-through. Granger
tests point the arrow out of markets into macro data, not in.

The reconciling mechanism is that prices are the *aggregation* of information,
not a delayed reaction to it. A dataset of world events can explain what already
happened and cannot tell you what happens next, because the thing being measured
already contains everyone's best guess about next.

The practical reading of this repo: it is a **causal-attribution and
risk-measurement dataset**, not a forecasting one. Good questions to ask it are
"what channel did this move come through", "how coupled are these two assets
right now", "what regime are we in", "how large is a 1.5-SD stress month". Bad
question: "what goes up next month."

## Caveats

- Monthly frequency only. Intraday and daily reactions to events are real and
  invisible here — a GPR spike can move oil 4% in a day and be flat over the
  month. The null result is about *monthly averages*, not about event impact.
- Equity proxy is Nasdaq Composite, which is tech-heavy and more volatile than
  a broad index. Conclusions about *predictability* are unlikely to change;
  specific coefficient magnitudes would.
- The event-study threshold (1.5 SD over a trailing 60-month window) is one
  choice among many. It was not tuned — but nothing here was corrected for the
  choice of threshold either.
- Granger causality is predictive precedence, not causation. "Equities
  Granger-cause sentiment" means equities carry the information first, not that
  the market causes people to feel a certain way.
- US-centric. `euro_area_drivers` is in the panel but the equity, credit and
  positioning series are all American.
